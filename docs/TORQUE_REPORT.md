## 6. Effect of the lowered velocity gain (walk)

The sim uses velocity gain 0.3 (`config.JOINT_VELOCITY_GAIN`, sim only). The same walk with PyBullet's default gain of 1.0:

| joint | peak N*m (gain 0.3) | peak N*m (gain 1.0) | RMS N*m (gain 0.3) | RMS N*m (gain 1.0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| coxa | 0.72 | 0.98 | 0.23 | 0.23 |
| femur | 0.75 | 0.91 | 0.23 | 0.25 |
| tibia | 0.70 | 1.07 | 0.17 | 0.17 |

With the default gain the walk peaks are up to **1.54x** the lowered-gain peaks (tibia). So the lowered gain UNDERSTATES the peak torque in section 1; allow for that on top of the safety factor.

## Limits of these numbers (read before using them)

- The sim servo is an ideal position motor: **no gear friction, no backlash, no compliance, no supply sag, no temperature effects.** A real servo needs more torque for the same motion; the safety factor is the margin for that, not a guarantee.
- The sim uses a **lowered velocity gain** (0.3, sim only). It damps the joints less than a real servo's own friction would, and it changes the torque readings (section 6 shows by how much).
- All masses are **placeholders**; torque scales roughly with mass (section 4).
- `getJointState` reports the torque the motor model applied after the cap; with the cap raised that is the torque the motion asked for.
- One flat floor, one friction value, no pushes or slopes. Add margin for them.
